Semester 1

  • Fundamentals of Music Technology — 12 hours

    This course introduces the essential tools, concepts and practices of sound and music technology. Students explore analog and digital audio systems, studio equipment, audio interfaces, signal routing, digital audio workstations, MIDI and OSC, and audio file formats. They also learn to record, edit, analyze and manipulate sound using established software tools.

    The course introduces computational musicology and AI-assisted audio analysis and processing, providing students from different academic backgrounds with a common practical and technical foundation for the rest of the program.

  • Computer Music Techniques — 12 hours

    This course introduces the principal techniques and programming environments used in computer music, including Pure Data, Faust, Max/MSP, SuperCollider and ChucK. Through practical experimentation with Pure Data and Faust, students explore signal flow, sound synthesis, audio effects, musical control and the design of interactive sound processes.

    The course emphasizes transferable concepts and the creative combination of synthesis and processing techniques, including the critical use of AI-assisted programming tools.

  • Interactive Audio Processing and Sound Design — 18 hours

    This course develops professional skills in interactive sound processing and sound design using Max/MSP. Students design real-time audio processing systems, reactive patches, musical control interfaces and sound transformations for live electronics, mixed music and interactive installations.

    Particular attention is paid to the practice of computer music assistance, including collaboration with artists, the integration of external devices and software, rehearsal and performance workflows, technical reliability, debugging and the documentation and preservation of electronic works.

  • Composition Forum — 21 hours

    This seminar is organized in partnership with GRAME-CNCM. Each year, an invited composer leads the course, exploring a specific musical aesthetic through their artistic practice, compositional approaches and creative processes. While primarily theoretical and analytical, the seminar is closely connected to the practical and technical components of the master's program. Students are encouraged to explore, adapt and apply the compositional techniques and artistic concepts discussed in the seminar to their own creative and technological projects throughout the curriculum.

  • Acoustics and Psychoacoustics — 24 hours

    This course provides the physical, mathematical and perceptual foundations required to understand sound production, propagation, capture, representation and reproduction. Students learn about acoustic waves, resonance, sound levels, room acoustics, electroacoustic transducers and digital audio, as well as the physiology of hearing, psychoacoustic phenomena and spatial sound perception in humans and other species.

    Lectures are combined with experimental work involving acoustic measurements, signal analysis and controlled listening. Students learn to relate measurable acoustic properties to auditory experience and to critically evaluate computational and AI-assisted acoustic tools in contexts ranging from music technology to bioacoustics.

  • Human-Computer Interaction — 32 hours

    This course explores the design of physical interfaces and interactive systems for music, sound and the arts. Students learn to program microcontrollers, build basic electronic circuits, interface sensors, capture gestures and map physical actions to digital media. Applications include digital musical instruments, controllers and interactive installations, with an introduction to AI-assisted gesture recognition and prototyping.

    A dedicated digital fabrication component introduces 3D modeling with Blender, 3D printing and laser cutting, enabling students to design and manufacture the physical components and enclosures of their interactive systems.

  • Introduction to Computer Programming — 24 hours

    This course introduces computational thinking and fundamental programming concepts through Python, with an emphasis on scientific computing, data manipulation, visualization and audio applications. Students develop skills in structuring programs, using libraries, processing datasets and testing and documenting their code.

    An introduction to C++ establishes the foundations of compiled programming, memory management and real-time constraints. AI-assisted development is integrated throughout the course, with particular emphasis on understanding, evaluating and correcting generated code rather than relying on it uncritically.

  • Audio Systems — 22 hours

    This course examines how analog and digital audio systems are structured and how their hardware and software components interact. Topics include digital audio representation, processor architectures, real-time software, audio drivers and frameworks, plug-in formats, digital audio interfaces, clocking, synchronization, networked audio and control protocols.

    Through practical programming experiments and architectural case studies, students learn to design, analyze and evaluate complete audio systems, identify technical constraints and make informed decisions when developing audio software with or without AI assistance.

  • Interdisciplinary Aesthetic Seminar — 15 hours

    This seminar is shared between multiple masters program in the Arts school at UJM. It focuses on Aesthetic in the Arts. Its content changes yearly.

  • Methodology

    This course introduces the methodologies and working practices needed to undertake academic, artistic and technical projects at master's level. Students develop approaches to identifying relevant sources, formulating questions, organizing their work, documenting their processes and communicating results critically and clearly.

  • Language Course — 18 hours

    Students can choose from a range of language courses, including French as a Foreign Language (FLE) for international students.

  • Symposium Report

    Students are required to attend a one-day academic or professional symposium and write a short report about it. The symposium is selected by each student in consultation with their academic advisor.

Semester 2

  • Audio Digital Signal Processing — 24 hours

    This course introduces the mathematical and algorithmic foundations of digital audio signal processing. Students study discrete-time systems, convolution, Fourier analysis, the FFT, the Z-transform, digital filters and sampling-rate conversion, and apply these concepts to sound synthesis, effects and audio analysis.

    Numerical experimentation and practical implementations in Python and Faust connect theory to real-world audio applications. The course provides a foundation for real-time audio programming and for understanding and evaluating data-driven signal processing approaches.

  • AI for Digital Signal Processing — 20 hours

    This course explores machine learning and neural network approaches to audio analysis, processing and synthesis. Students examine audio representations, training and inference, neural architectures and pretrained models, and their applications to sound classification, source separation, enhancement, neural synthesis and audio effects.

    Practical work with Python and machine learning frameworks introduces model development, evaluation and integration, including hybrid neural and conventional DSP approaches. Students critically assess the performance, controllability, computational requirements and limitations of these methods, including their suitability for real-time and embedded audio systems.

  • History of Electronic Music — 21 hours

    This course traces the development of electronic and electroacoustic music from early electrical instruments and recording technologies to contemporary computer music and digital practices. It explores musique concrète, electronic music studios, acousmatic and mixed music, synthesizers, experimental and popular electronic music, and the changing relationship between technological developments and musical aesthetics.

    Guided listening, analysis of representative works and accessible practical experiments help students understand the creative possibilities and constraints of the technologies that have shaped electronic music, regardless of their technical background.

  • 20th and 21st Century Musicology — 21 hours

    This research seminar explores musicological approaches to the music of the 20th and 21st centuries. Each year, the seminar focuses on a specific topic, examining musical practices, aesthetic movements and their historical, social and cultural contexts through critical discussion, analysis and engagement with current research.

  • Bioacoustics — 40 hours

    This module takes place within the framework of the Bioacoustics Winter School, organized by the ENES Bioacoustics Research Laboratory at Jean Monnet University. Bringing together an international community of researchers and students, the winter school explores the production, propagation and perception of biological sounds, with particular emphasis on acoustic communication in humans and animals.

    Combining lectures, practical sessions and experimental approaches, the program introduces students to bioacoustic research methods, sound recording and analysis, experimental design and data interpretation. It also explores applications in ecology, animal behavior, biodiversity monitoring and environmental acoustics. The specific content varies each year according to the scientific program and invited researchers.

    For SMT students, this module provides an opportunity to apply their knowledge of acoustics, signal processing and audio technologies to research questions beyond the traditional fields of music and audio engineering.

  • Electronics and Embedded Systems for Audio — 20 hours

    This course introduces the design and implementation of custom electronic hardware for audio applications. Students explore analog and digital audio circuitry, power supplies, audio codecs, embedded processing architectures and communication interfaces, and learn to design electronic schematics and printed circuit boards using electronic design automation tools.

    Practical work connects circuit design, PCB development, embedded programming, real-time audio DSP and hardware testing. Students develop the skills needed to prototype and evaluate audio devices and critically assess AI-assisted hardware and software design solutions.

  • Audio Studio Techniques — 20 hours

    This course introduces the principles and practices of sound engineering in home and professional recording studios. Students explore studio equipment, signal routing, microphone selection and placement, recording techniques, digital audio workstations, mixing, mastering and professional audio delivery standards.

    Through hands-on studio sessions and recording projects, students develop practical production skills, critical listening abilities and an understanding of collaborative studio workflows. The course also examines the role and limitations of AI-assisted audio production tools.

  • Immersive Audio — 16 hours

    This course explores the principles, technologies and creative applications of spatial and immersive audio. Students study multichannel panning, binaural audio, Ambisonics, Wave Field Synthesis, microphone arrays, immersive audio formats and spatial sound rendering, building on their knowledge of acoustics and auditory perception.

    Practical experimentation with multichannel systems and spatial audio software introduces the capture, processing, reproduction and evaluation of immersive sound scenes, as well as spatialization as a parameter of musical composition and sound design.

  • Audio Programming — 12 hours

    This practical course integrates the technical knowledge acquired throughout the first year through the development of complete audio applications. Students translate functional requirements into software architectures, implement real-time audio processing using C++ and Faust, and integrate DSP algorithms, user interfaces, control protocols and, where appropriate, neural audio models.

    The course emphasizes project-based development, AI-assisted programming, testing, performance evaluation and software documentation, preparing students to undertake more ambitious independent projects in the second year.

  • Project and Critical Feedback — 8 hours

    This module provides students with an opportunity to develop an individual or collaborative project of their choice, drawing on the knowledge and skills acquired throughout the first year of the master's program. Projects may be technical, artistic or combine both dimensions, reflecting students' interests and professional aspirations.

    The module is structured around a series of critical feedback sessions during which students present their progress, discuss conceptual and technical challenges, receive constructive feedback from instructors and peers, and refine their work. Particular emphasis is placed on autonomy, creativity, experimentation and critical reflection.

    Conceived as a preparatory experience for the third-semester Capstone Project, the module culminates in a presentation and collective discussion of the completed projects.

  • Language Course — 18 hours

    Students can choose from a range of language courses, including French as a Foreign Language (FLE) for international students.

  • Symposium Report

    Students are required to attend a one-day academic or professional symposium and write a short report about it. The symposium is selected by each student in consultation with their academic advisor.

Semester 3

  • Composition Forum — 21 hours

    This seminar is organized in partnership with GRAME-CNCM. Each year, an invited composer leads the course, exploring a specific musical aesthetic through their artistic practice, compositional approaches and creative processes. While primarily theoretical and analytical, the seminar is closely connected to the practical and technical components of the master's program. Students are encouraged to explore, adapt and apply the compositional techniques and artistic concepts discussed in the seminar to their own creative and technological projects throughout the curriculum.

  • AI, Sound, and Music — 12 hours

    This course explores the creative, technical and cultural dimensions of artificial intelligence in sound and music. Students examine musical representations, generative models, AI-assisted composition and sound design, music analysis and interactive machine learning, with practical experimentation using pretrained models and their integration into artistic workflows.

    Critical discussion addresses artistic agency, authorship, training data, cultural representation and the impact of AI on musical practices and creative industries. Students develop the ability to evaluate and adapt AI technologies in accordance with their artistic and technical objectives.

  • Technical Foundations of Virtual Reality — 30 hours

    Designed for the artists, filmmakers, and designers of the Erasmus Mundus Joint Master in Digital Creativity Arts & Science (EMJMD DigiCrea), Technical Foundations of Virtual Reality opens the black box of Virtual and Extended Reality to put technology at the service of creation. Rather than treating real-time 3D tools as opaque software, the course equips students with the technical grounding needed to make informed artistic and production decisions. Adopting a hands-on, browser-based approach, it introduces Three.js as early as the second session—allowing students to immediately visualize, test, and prototype scenes, lighting, materials, and 3D models live in their browser as each new concept unfolds.

    Across seven sessions combining lectures and interactive workshops, the curriculum progresses from foundational Extended Reality concepts, including the reality-virtuality continuum, 6DoF interaction, and the XR hardware ecosystem, to the inner workings of modern GPU architectures and the complete graphics rendering pipeline that enables stereoscopic rendering at high frame rates. Building on this foundation, students take direct creative control of every pixel through custom GLSL shader programming and step inside their own scenes using the WebXR Device API across VR headsets, AR smartphones, and MR passthrough devices. The course concludes by looking under the hood of Three.js, introducing low-level GPU programming with WebGL2 and next-generation WebGPU and WGSL compute shaders.

  • Artistic Project Management — 10 hours

    This course introduces the organizational, administrative, financial and strategic aspects of developing and managing artistic and cultural projects. It explores project planning, budgeting, funding opportunities, cultural policies, communication strategies and professional networks. Shared with a master's program specializing in music management and business, it encourages collaboration between students with complementary artistic, technical and managerial backgrounds, preparing them to develop and implement projects within the cultural and creative industries.

  • Introduction to TouchDesigner — 18 hours

    Introduction to the TouchDesigner programming environment (derivative.ca) dedicated to real-time image processing. The course will focus more specifically on the production of interactive content and its connection to gesture-based interfaces.

  • History and Theory of the Arts — 18 hours

    This course focuses on the Internet as a space of collaborative, critical and grassroot inquiry allowing specific forms of creation at the crossroads of art, activism and research. The digital networks open up new possibilities to share, gather and analyze information that can be explored from an artistic and engaged point of view. This course will present different fields of counter-investigation shaped by on-line contents: OSINT inquiries, desktop movies, net art, forensic art, screen walks… These examples will be framed within the recent history of the Internet and the struggle to make it an environment as democratic as possible. In the era of the corporate platforms and the so-called “AI revolution”, one of the main duties of the digital art could be to challenge the access to knowledge and its social infrastructures.

  • Graduate Proseminar — 7 hours

    Organized by Graduate+ Arts, this interdisciplinary research seminar brings together established researchers, doctoral candidates and master's students from the arts, humanities and social sciences. Structured around a common research theme, it combines academic presentations, roundtable discussions and active student participation. The seminar introduces students to interdisciplinary research practices, encourages critical reflection on research methodologies and fosters dialogue across disciplinary boundaries.

  • Capstone Project and Master's Thesis

    The Capstone Project constitutes the culmination of the master's program, allowing students to mobilize the artistic, scientific and technical knowledge acquired throughout their studies. Students independently develop an ambitious project in sound and music technology, which may be engineering-oriented, artistic, research-oriented or combine these dimensions.

    Under the supervision of an academic advisor, students define their objectives, establish a methodology, develop and evaluate their work, and position it within its broader scientific, artistic or professional context. Regular critical feedback sessions support the development of their projects.

    The project is accompanied by a master's thesis documenting the research process, relevant literature, methodological choices, technical or artistic contributions, results and critical reflections. The module culminates in an oral defense and a presentation of the project.

  • Language Course — 18 hours

    Students can choose from a range of language courses, including French as a Foreign Language (FLE) for international students.

Semester 4

  • 6 Months Internship

    The final semester is dedicated to a six-month internship in a professional or academic setting relevant to sound and music technology. Students apply their artistic, scientific and technical skills to real-world projects within an organization such as an audio technology company, research laboratory, cultural institution or artistic production environment. The internship provides an opportunity to develop professional autonomy, deepen specialist expertise and prepare for entry into the international job market or further academic research.
